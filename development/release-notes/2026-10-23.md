# Release Notes: 23 October 2026

## 1. A Plan for Devices

In this update the domain records how its networks will one day leave the simulator: compiled through calx to GPUs, neuromorphic chips and quantum processors, with the simulator's checks as the reference every device must meet ([idea](../ideas/2026-10-23-spiking-networks-on-devices.md)). It is a plan; nothing of it is built.

## 2. A Quantum Neuron

In this update the simulator has its first quantum neuron: a driven two-level emitter whose detected photons are its spikes, followed along exact quantum trajectories, so that a network of them averages to their master equation. Input spikes are pulses that rotate its state. Its photons match the closed-form waiting time, and twenty thousand trajectories match the master equation ([theory](../../theory/spiking-networks.md), §8).

## 3. Seven New Neurons

In this update the simulator gains seven neuron models, bringing it to twenty-one. Two come from deep spiking networks: the adaptive LIF neuron, now integrated exactly and so stable for any parameters, and the balanced resonate-and-fire neuron of 2024, which fires without a reset and answers to its own frequency. The generalised integrate-and-fire neuron with escape noise is the model fitted to recorded cells. Four classic models show how a neuron begins to fire: Izhikevich's model in physical units, Wang and Buzsáki's fast-spiking interneuron, Morris and Lecar's fibre and FitzHugh and Nagumo's model. Each is checked against the bifurcation that defines its class, which the theory note now explains ([theory](../../theory/spiking-networks.md), §7), and broad spikes are counted once.

## 4. Networks of Spiking Neurons

In this update the domain gains its first C++: a simulator of spiking neural networks ([module](../../snn/README.md), [decision 0002](../decisions/0002-spiking-networks-in-cpp.md)). It offers fourteen neuron models, from the leaky integrate-and-fire neuron to the adaptive exponential, Izhikevich and Hodgkin–Huxley models, and synapses that learn by pair or triplet STDP or change over short times. A network can be written in C++ or as a JSON file with its quantities in units, and run from the command line.

Where a model is linear between spikes it is integrated exactly. A run gives the same spikes on any number of threads. Brunel's classic network of 12,500 neurons simulates a second of activity in 2.6 seconds on a laptop. The simulations agree with the theory: Brunel's network fires within 0.4 % of its mean-field rate, and quadratic integrate-and-fire neurons within 2 % of Montbrió, Pazó and Roxin's exact mean field.

A survey of recent models, learning rules and quantum neuromorphic models sets out what comes next.

## 5. Signals of the Brain

In this update the domain can read brain recordings in EDF, EDF+ (with their annotations), BDF and BrainVision formats, and filter them and measure their spectra and bands. It can carry EEG and MEG from the cortex to the sensors and back: a three-shell head model, Sarvas's magnetic field, and minimum-norm, dSPM and sLORETA estimates of the sources. It can read fMRI maps through the cortical surface ([theory](../../theory/forward-and-inverse.md)). On a real recording it finds Berger's alpha rhythm where it should be.
